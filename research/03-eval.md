## 0. Headlines that change the plan

1. **Promptfoo is now part of OpenAI.** Its README says "Promptfoo is now part of OpenAI. Promptfoo remains open source and MIT licensed." (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/promptfoo/promptfoo/main/README.md). The repo has 26,452 stars and was pushed the day of this research.
2. **OpenAI is shutting down its Evals platform.** Evals go read-only 2026-10-31 and the dashboard and API shut down 2026-11-30 (https://platform.openai.com/docs/deprecations, section "2026-06-03: Evals platform"). OpenAI's migration cookbook points customers at Promptfoo and says no export feature is needed because you "manually recreate" the eval (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/openai-cookbook/main/examples/evaluation/moving-from-openai-evals-to-promptfoo.md). Consequence: customers with OpenAI-hosted evals are being forced to re-platform this month, and the default landing spot is a tool owned by the incumbent vendor. A vendor-neutral replay tool has a real opening. A GLM-focused tool must be neutral-by-construction (it can run any provider as incumbent or candidate).
3. **OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions moved repos and are still "Development".** The old page in `open-telemetry/semantic-conventions` says "GenAI semantic conventions have moved to open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai" (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-spans.md). The client-inference doc marks status Development. Do not treat it as stable; pin to a commit and write an adapter.
4. **Humanloop is gone** (secondary): Anthropic hired the team in Aug 2025 and the platform shut down 2025-09-08; IP was reportedly not part of the deal (https://wandb.ai/wandb_fc/product-announcements-fc/reports/Humanloop-is-sunsetting-Migrate-to-Weights-Biases-as-an-alternative--VmlldzoxMzk4ODc1Nw, W&B is a competitor so treat as marketing). Anthropic's own surface is the Console "Evaluate" tab.
5. **Vendors publish exactly the kind of report we will produce, and it has statistical gaps.** Together AI's GLM-5.3 vs GLM-5.3 Flash post (2026-08-28) gives pass@1 with a +/- figure, cost per task, solves per $100 and a cascade result, but no paired test or CI on the difference (https://together.ai/blog/glm-5-3-vs-glm-5-3-flash-on-deepswe-cost-coding-and-routing). See section 6.

### Where the market leaves room (the gap)

- No tool I read takes **existing production traces from an incumbent (Claude/OpenAI)** and replays them across **model variants and hosts** (e.g. GLM-5.3 low/high/max, Flash, FlashX, Z.ai vs Together vs a customer-hosted endpoint) with a **paired comparison and CI per task type**.
- Langfuse, Braintrust, LangSmith and Phoenix show cost and latency next to scores but, from the pages read, do not state significance or CIs on the difference. Only Inspect has first-class SE/CI/cluster tooling, and it is benchmark-shaped.
- Nobody offered **cache-hit forensics** (why did cost differ from list-price math) or **verbosity forensics** (output tokens per task, reasoning tokens, length-controlled judging) in the material I read. Together's post notes the Flash "runs the same number of steps" but it is a one-off analysis, not a tool.
- **Spend/loop guard** during replay (budget cap, max turns, repeated-tool-call detector): not seen in any of them.
- **Self-contained static artifact** an FDE can hand over: Langfuse/Braintrust/LangSmith are accounts and seats; promptfoo has a local viewer; none emits a single offline HTML with the raw per-item data.
- Promptfoo-under-OpenAI plus OpenAI Evals shutdown on 2026-11-30 creates demand for a neutral, local-first option right now.

### Proposed minimal normalized schema ("SwitchTrace v0", one JSONL line per model call)

Design rules: input tokens are always stored in the OpenAI/OTel convention (total, cache included) with explicit cache fields; the replay input is the full message list and tool definitions; provenance fields let us trace any number back to a source row.

  "call_id": "string, stable (source request_id or hash of source row)",
  "trace_id": "string, groups calls of one task/session",
  "parent_call_id": "string|null",
  "seq": 0,
  "ts_start": "RFC3339",
  "task_type": "string|null  (assigned by customer tag or classifier; required for per-task-type reporting)",
  "source": {"kind": "otel|langfuse|openai|anthropic|litellm|claude_code|golden", "ref": "file#row/id", "adapter_version": "x.y"},

## 3. Statistics method

Primary source: Evan Miller, "Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations", arXiv 2411.00640 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640, PDF read). The task statement calls it Anthropic's; the text I read names only the author, so I did not verify the affiliation.

What the paper recommends (all read in the PDF):
1. Treat questions as drawn from a super-population; compute SE of the mean via the CLT, `SE = sqrt(Var(s)/n)`; 95% CI = mean +/- 1.96 SE. Bootstrapping is "unnecessary unless a complicated sampling scheme or estimator is being used".
2. **Clustered standard errors** when items are related (it reports clustered SE up to 3x the naive SE on DROP). For us: multiple calls from the same session/ticket/customer are clusters; the trace-derived golden set is full of them.
3. Variance reduction by **resampling each question K times** and averaging per question (K=2 cuts that variance component by 1/3, K=4 by 1/2 in its worked example). Do not pool all K*N answers (inconsistent). Do not lower temperature to reduce variance.
4. **Paired differences**: `s_{A-B,i} = s_{A,i} - s_{B,i}`, `SE_paired = sqrt(Var(s_{A-B})/n)`; variance falls by `2 Cov(A,B)/n` so it is a "free" reduction when models agree on which items are hard; report the difference, its SE, the CI and the score correlation (its Table 5 format). Clustered paired variant given as Eq. 8.
5. **Power analysis**: sample-size formula for a target minimum detectable effect, with paired design and K resamples.

Recommended method for switch-eval (my synthesis; method choices beyond the paper are marked as such):
- **Unit of analysis**: item (golden example or trace), cluster = session/ticket/customer ID. Score per item = mean over K replays (default K=3 for stochastic/tool-use tasks).
- **Headline per task type**: delta = candidate - incumbent on the same items; paired SE with clustering; 95% CI. Also report the correlation.
- **Binary pass/fail items**: report paired difference with CI, plus **McNemar's exact test** on discordant pairs (b = incumbent-only passes, c = candidate-only passes) as the significance cue. With K>1, McNemar needs one binary per item (e.g., majority) so prefer the CLT paired CI on per-item means as primary and McNemar as secondary. (McNemar is standard; not from the Miller paper.)
- **Small n or skewed metrics (cost, latency, tokens)**: percentile **paired bootstrap** over clusters (e.g., 10,000 resamples) for ratios and medians, since cost per task is heavy-tailed. Inspect uses bootstrap_stderr with 1,000 samples as a default, and Wilson intervals for binary rates (https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/metrics.html). Use Wilson for single-model pass rates near 0 or 1.
- **Decision framing for the CTO**: pre-declare a non-inferiority margin (e.g., "candidate no worse than incumbent by more than 3 points"); verdict is one of: *non-inferior* (lower CI bound above -margin), *inferior* (upper bound below -margin), *inconclusive - need N more items* (use the power formula to say how many). This is more useful than "p<0.05".
- **Multiple comparisons**: variants x task types creates many tests. Say so on the report; apply Holm or just label task-type CIs as unadjusted. (Method choice, not from sources read.)
- **Pass^k for agents**: tau-bench style reliability (`pass^k`) is a different quantity from pass@k; Artificial Analysis uses pass^5 on one agentic eval (https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking). Report both when K>1 for tool-use tasks. Together's GLM post shows why it matters: Flash pass@1 63.4% vs 69.0% but pass@4 85.0% vs 87.6%.

### LLM-as-judge bias controls

Evidence: Zheng et al., "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) studies position, verbosity and self-enhancement bias and limited reasoning ability; reports GPT-4 judge agreement with humans above 80% once biases are handled; in the table I saw via search, Claude-v1 chose the first answer in 75% of cases under the default prompt (secondary snippet). I did not read the verbosity and self-enhancement magnitudes.

Controls to build in (design choices; the first three are supported by the cited paper and by Artificial Analysis practice):
1. **Position swap**: for pairwise grading run both orders; count a win only if consistent, else tie. Log the inconsistency rate as a judge-quality metric.
2. **Judge panel from different families, none from the candidate or incumbent family** where possible. Artificial Analysis uses three judges from different labs "reducing bias toward submissions from the same model or model family" and blinds the pairing (methodology page above). Since the candidate is GLM and the incumbent is Claude or OpenAI, a GPT/Claude/Gemini panel includes the incumbent's family; surface this explicitly, and allow a non-participating judge.
3. **Prefer rubric/reference-based checks over free preference**; use deterministic task checks (schema validity, exact fields, SQL execution result, tool-call arguments) wherever the customer data allows, and use the judge only for what remains.
4. **Length control**: report judge win rate stratified by length ratio or regress score on output-length difference; flag cases where the more verbose answer wins. (Inferred from the verbosity-bias literature; magnitudes not read.)
5. **Blind**: strip model names, formatting tells, and reasoning traces before judging.
6. **Calibrate against humans**: have the customer SME label 50-100 items, report judge-human agreement (Cohen's kappa) per task type, and show the judge's own CI. Langfuse's judge docs also say to calibrate against labeled examples before relying on a judge (https://langfuse.com/docs/evaluation/evaluation-methods/llm-as-a-judge).
7. **Judge noise**: judge scores are another resampled variable; temperature fixed, run twice and report disagreement. Cache judge verdicts keyed by (judge, rubric version, content hash) for reproducibility.

## 4. How vendors present migration results to buyers

- **Together AI playbook** (2026-09-16, https://together.ai/blog/migrating-from-closed-to-open-source-models): five stages discover / evaluate / adapt / decide / production. Splits evaluation into *accuracy* and *performance* (cost and latency). Says "benchmarks are directionally useful, true tests are done with real data", replay your own traffic, and "if you don't have data to replay you're not blocked": size by input/output size and cache hit rate; names LiteLLM as a gateway to collect those numbers. "Up to 70% cost reduction" quoted as tokens-per-dollar. They promise a deeper follow-up on sizing and replay. Together also sells "Evaluations" (LLM-judge benchmark with open judge models, https://together.ai/blog/introducing-together-evaluations, 2025-07-28) so it is a partial competitor on the judging step but is host-locked.
- **Together GLM-5.3 vs Flash** (https://together.ai/blog/glm-5-3-vs-glm-5-3-flash-on-deepswe-cost-coding-and-routing): table with pass@1 +/- SE, average cost per task ($3.99 vs $0.24), "solves per $100" (17 vs 264), average output tokens, average steps, 113 tasks x 4 trials; pass@k curves; consistency classes (wall/flaky/solid); per-domain wins; a "collateral damage" regression rate; and a cascade result (80.9% at $1.70 per task). This is the presentation style buyers have now seen. Missing: paired CI on deltas, cluster adjustment.
- **Together Kimi K2.7 Code vs Claude Fable 5** (https://together.ai/blog/kimi-k2-7-code-vs-claude-fable-5): 12 landing pages, "94% less" cost, quality "within a few points", with every output published for inspection. Small n; headline cost ratio with qualitative quality.
- **Vendor-reported case studies** (secondary): Checkr via Predibase (fine-tuned Llama-3-8B, claims 5x cheaper, 30x faster than GPT-4 on the hardest 2% of cases; gated whitepaper). I did not find primary Fireworks, Baseten, Databricks or Mistral migration case studies in the two searches I ran; treat that as "not found", not "none exist".
- **Cost-trap warning** (secondary, single-author blog): reasoning models billed for reasoning tokens can cost more per request than the model replaced despite a lower headline rate (https://mecanik.dev/en/posts/moving-off-openai-open-weight-switch-cost/). This is exactly what the verbosity forensics should quantify for GLM `max` vs `high` vs `low`.

### What a buyer/CTO expects in the report (synthesis)

1. **One-page verdict per task type**: recommendation (switch / switch with routing / do not switch), the paired quality delta with 95% CI against the non-inferiority margin, and the cost ratio. Traffic-light rendering is fine if the CI is also shown.
2. **Quality / cost / latency triple** per task type and per candidate (GLM-5.3 low/high/max, Flash, FlashX; host A/B): quality delta with CI; cost per successful task and per 1,000 requests (not just per token); p50/p95 latency and TTFT. Together-style "solves per $100" is the unit buyers have seen.
3. **Cost reconciliation**: incumbent cost from their own spend logs vs our recomputation; list-price assumptions and price-table date; cached vs uncached input split; projected monthly spend at their volume with a range.
4. **Cache-hit forensics**: observed cache-hit rate on the incumbent vs the candidate on replay, which prompt prefixes are cacheable, what the savings would be at the observed rate, and the cache-write cost. Artificial Analysis already insists on live-measured cache hit rates over one-off measurements.
5. **Verbosity forensics**: output tokens and reasoning tokens per task (median, p95), length ratio vs incumbent, share of calls hitting `max_tokens` / `finish_reason=length`, loop/retry signatures; show quality-vs-effort curve (low/high/max) and cost at each point.
6. **Failure taxonomy with examples**: top regressions (worst N items by delta), grouped by cause (format, tool-call args, refusal, truncation, language), each with side-by-side outputs. Langfuse's own guidance is that average improvement can hide a regression on a critical case, so list "critical item" regressions separately.
7. **Reliability**: pass^k or run-to-run variance for agentic tasks.
8. **Routing / cascade recommendation**: for each task type, parity -> use cheap model; otherwise cascade with verifier (Together's cascade result is the template; RouteLLM's threshold calibration is the prior art).
9. **Judge audit**: judge model(s), rubric, position-swap inconsistency rate, human-agreement stats, self-preference caveat.
10. **Methodology and limits**: n, clusters, K, sampling params, dates, price table version, what was not tested (long context, safety, multilingual), data-handling note (what left the customer's boundary).
11. **Spend/loop guard log**: how much the evaluation itself cost, what was capped or aborted.
12. **Reproducibility bundle**: config, SwitchTrace inputs hash, raw per-item results (JSONL), and a static HTML dashboard.
